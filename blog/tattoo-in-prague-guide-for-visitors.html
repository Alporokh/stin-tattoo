Getting a Tattoo in Prague: A Guide for International Visitors

Prague is a city people like to take a piece of home with them. Sometimes that means a photograph of Charles Bridge, a print from a small gallery or something found in an antique shop. And sometimes it means a tattoo.

If you're visiting the Czech capital and thinking about getting a tattoo in Prague, you probably have a few practical questions first. Do you need to book weeks in advance? Can you talk to the tattoo artist in English? Can you just walk in? And how do you look after a fresh tattoo when you still have several days of sightseeing ahead?

Here's what you should know before booking a tattoo during your trip.

Can tourists get a tattoo in Prague?

Yes. You don't need to live in the Czech Republic to book a tattoo here, and tattooing someone who's only in town for a few days is completely normal for a Prague tattoo studio.

Prague has a large international community and welcomes millions of visitors every year, so most studios in the centre are used to clients who fly in, get tattooed and fly home.

At STIN Tattoo Studio, we work in English and Czech, so you can discuss your idea, placement, size and aftercare without a language barrier. The studio is in Malá Strana, Prague 1, a short walk from Charles Bridge and some of the city's best-known sights.

You can arrange your tattoo before your trip or message us once you're already in Prague.

Should I book my tattoo before coming to Prague?

If getting tattooed is part of your travel plan, book ahead. It's the only way to be sure you get a slot with the artist you want, especially for a custom design or a larger piece.

Having time before the appointment also gives the artist a chance to understand your idea and prepare the design properly. When you contact a tattoo shop in Prague, it helps to send:

  • a short description of your idea;
  • one or two reference images, if you have them;
  • the approximate size;
  • the placement you have in mind;
  • the dates you'll be in Prague.

You don't need to arrive with a perfectly finished concept. Sometimes you have a clear image in your head. Sometimes you only know you want something botanical, ornamental or connected to your trip. Both are fine.

At STIN, custom designs are drawn around the person who'll actually wear the tattoo, not copied from an image someone found online.

Can I get a walk-in tattoo in Prague?

Often, yes. STIN welcomes walk-ins whenever there's a free slot, and this works best for smaller tattoos and ideas that don't need much preparation.

Availability changes from day to day, though. If you only have one free afternoon during your stay, send us a message first rather than leaving it to chance.

For a larger or more detailed custom piece, booking in advance is usually the better choice.

How do I choose the right tattoo studio in Prague?

Look at the artist's actual portfolio in the style you want, not just the studio's Instagram grid or its list of services. Consistent, clean work in your style tells you far more than a "best tattoo in Prague" headline.

Different artists have different strengths, and studios also host visiting guest artists for short spells. At STIN, Anna works across fine line, floral, ornamental, graphic, engraving, traditional, micro realism, blackwork and lettering. Her focus is clean composition, detail and designs that sit naturally on the body.

If you're looking for a fine line tattoo in Prague, for example, look for real fine line pieces in the portfolio, ideally including healed ones. The same goes for ornamental, floral or detailed illustrative work.

It's also worth checking a few practical things before you commit:

  • Does the artist reply clearly and answer your questions in a language you're comfortable with?
  • Are recent Google reviews from other visitors, not just locals?
  • Is the studio clean, and are needles opened in front of you?

Your tattoo is going home with you. Choosing a tattoo artist in Prague whose work genuinely feels right is worth more than walking into the first studio you happen to pass.

What tattoo should I get in Prague?

Whatever you'll still be happy to see on your skin long after the trip. There's no rule that a travel tattoo has to be a miniature Charles Bridge, unless that's exactly what you want.

A tattoo from Prague can be connected to the city without being an obvious souvenir. It could be a small architectural detail you noticed walking through Malá Strana, a symbol from the trip, a botanical motif, a date, a word, an abstract shape or simply a design you decided to get while you were here.

And it doesn't need to have anything to do with Prague at all. Travel sometimes gives you the time and distance to finally get the tattoo you've been thinking about for years.

So the better question isn't "What tattoo should a tourist get in Prague?" It's "What would I still love to have when this trip is a distant memory?"

Are fine line and small tattoos a good choice for travellers?

Yes, and they're the most popular choice for visitors. Small and fine line tattoos usually fit into one shorter session, which makes them much easier to plan around a city break.

Fine line work suits delicate botanical motifs, symbols, minimal figures, small compositions and lettering.

But small doesn't mean effortless. A tiny tattoo still needs the right proportions, the right placement and enough space for the lines to stay readable as skin changes over the years. An experienced artist may suggest making a design slightly larger, simplifying a detail or moving it.

That's not about changing your idea. It's about making sure your idea works as a tattoo.

How do I prepare for my tattoo appointment?

Come rested, fed and sober. Prague has excellent beer; save it for another evening.

Drink water, eat a proper meal beforehand and avoid alcohol before the session.

Wear something comfortable that gives the artist easy access to the area. If you're getting a tattoo on your thigh, tight jeans aren't the most practical choice.

If you have a medical condition, allergies or anything else that could affect tattooing or healing, tell your artist before the appointment.

And don't squeeze the session between a 20,000-step sightseeing day and a night out. Your body will appreciate a little breathing room.

When during my Prague trip should I get tattooed?

Towards the end, if you can. A fresh tattoo needs to stay clean and protected while it heals, which doesn't mix well with packed sightseeing days, pools, saunas, hard workouts or hours in the summer sun.

Here's how the timing usually plays out:

If Prague is the first stop on a two-week holiday with beaches and swimming, getting tattooed on day one probably isn't the best plan. If it's your last weekend before flying home, things get much easier.

Tell your artist about your travel plans. They'll explain how to protect the tattoo on the journey and what to avoid.

Can I fly after getting a tattoo?

For most people, yes. Flying home with a fresh tattoo is manageable, as long as you treat it as the healing skin it is.

Follow the aftercare instructions your artist gives you, keep the area clean and avoid friction from tight clothing or luggage straps. Clothes that stick to a new tattoo can be uncomfortable and slow down healing.

On a long flight, comfort matters. A fresh tattoo right under a tight waistband or a seatbelt line can make several hours on a plane a lot less pleasant, so mention your flight when you discuss placement and timing.

Can I swim or go to a spa after getting a tattoo?

Not straight away. Dermatology guidance is to avoid swimming and the sun for about two weeks, and that includes pools, hot tubs, lakes and the sea.

This matters most if you're visiting in summer. Saunas and spas fall into the same category, so if your Prague itinerary includes a spa day, do it before your tattoo appointment.

After that, follow the healing instructions and wait until the tattoo has properly healed before going back to swimming.

How do I protect a new tattoo from the sun?

Keep it covered while it heals, and use sunscreen on it for good once it has. Daily sunscreen is what keeps tattoo colours bright over the years.

The same goes for black and grey work: sun is the main reason fine lines lose contrast and soften over time.

Can I get tattooed in English in Prague?

Yes. At STIN Tattoo Studio, consultations and tattoo sessions are in English as well as Czech.

Tattooing takes more conversation than people expect. You're not just saying "I want this picture here." You'll talk about scale, line weight, composition, placement, changes to the sketch and how the tattoo will sit on your body, and you need to understand the aftercare instructions when you leave.

That's why it's worth finding an English-speaking tattoo artist in Prague before you book.

So ask questions. Change your mind about the size. Ask to move the stencil. Look at it from different angles. A good tattoo appointment shouldn't feel rushed just because you're travelling.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a tattoo cost in Prague?

It depends on size, detail and placement, so there's no single price. Send us your idea, approximate size and placement, and we'll give you an estimate before you book.

How long does a small tattoo take?

Most small and fine line tattoos are done in a single session. The time also includes talking through the design and placing the stencil, so don't book it right before a tour or a train.

Can I bring my own design?

Yes. Bring references, sketches or photos. Anna will adapt the design so it works as a tattoo and fits your body, rather than copying an image one to one.

Can I get tattooed the same day I message you?

Sometimes. Walk-ins are welcome when there's a free slot, and small pieces are the easiest to fit in. Message us in the morning and we'll tell you what's possible that day.

How long does a new tattoo take to heal?

The surface usually settles within a few weeks, and daily washing and moisturising is typically needed for two to four weeks. Your artist will give you aftercare instructions for your specific tattoo.

What tattoo styles does STIN do?

Fine line, floral, ornamental, graphic, engraving, traditional, micro realism, blackwork and lettering. Every design is custom.

What should I do if something doesn't feel right while it's healing?

Message the studio first; we're happy to look at photos and advise, even after you've gone home. If you notice signs of infection such as increasing pain, swelling or heat, see a doctor.

Is it OK to get a tattoo on the last day before my flight?

Yes, that's often the best timing. Just avoid placements that will rub against a seatbelt, waistband or bag strap, and keep the aftercare products your artist recommends in your hand luggage.

Want to get a tattoo while you're in Prague?

Send us your idea before you arrive. Tell us what you'd like, where you're thinking of placing it, roughly how big it should be and which dates you'll be in Prague. References are welcome too.

We'll help you work out the rest.
