Tattoo Aftercare: A Complete Healing Guide

Číst česky →

Your tattoo is finished on the skin in a few hours. It finishes healing over several weeks, and most of that part is in your hands.

This is the guide we give our clients at STIN in Malá Strana, Prague. It covers the first days under the film, the daily routine after it comes off, what healing normally looks like week by week, what to avoid and for how long, and the few signs that mean you should see a doctor rather than wait and see.

The first four days: second skin

We cover every fresh tattoo with a breathable adhesive film, usually called second skin. Leave it on for four days and do not take it off early.

The film keeps the tattoo clean, lets the skin breathe and stops the tattoo catching on clothes and bed sheets. Underneath it you will see fluid collecting: a cloudy mix of plasma, a little blood and excess ink. The first time you see it, it looks alarming. It is normal and it means the film is doing its job.

Two things are not normal. If the film lifts at the edges so the seal is broken, or if fluid starts leaking out, take it off, wash the tattoo as described below, and carry on without it. Do not stick a new film on unless the studio gave you one and showed you how.

Taking the film off

Do it in the shower, under warm running water. Wet the edges first, then peel slowly and flat along the skin rather than pulling it up and away. Warm water softens the adhesive and the film comes off without dragging at the tattoo.

Then wash it properly:

  • Clean hands first, every single time.
  • Lukewarm water and a mild, fragrance-free soap.
  • Fingertips only. No washcloth, no sponge, no scrubbing.
  • Pat dry with a paper towel. Bath towels hold bacteria and leave fibres behind.
  • Let the skin air for a few minutes, then a thin layer of the cream we recommend.

The daily routine, weeks one to three

Wash, dry, cream. Two to three times a day, for about three weeks.

The word that matters is thin. A thick layer of cream traps moisture against the skin, softens the scab and keeps it wet, and soft scabs are how crisp lines turn blurry. The layer should sink in within a minute. If the tattoo looks shiny and greasy an hour later, you used too much.

The milky stage in weeks three and four catches almost everyone out. A layer of new skin has formed over the ink and it is not transparent yet, so the tattoo looks dull and washed out. It is not a bad heal. Do not judge your tattoo before week eight.

What is normal

  • Redness and swelling for the first few days.
  • Warmth around the area, easing after day three.
  • Clear or cloudy fluid under the film.
  • Peeling like sunburn in week two.
  • Intense itching, worst while it peels.
  • A dull, milky look in weeks three and four.
  • A few flakes of colour coming away with the skin.

What is not normal

See a doctor if you notice any of these:

  • Redness spreading outward from the tattoo, especially as a streak or line.
  • Pain or swelling getting worse after day three instead of better.
  • The area hot to the touch.
  • Thick yellow or green discharge, or a bad smell.
  • Fever, chills or feeling generally unwell.
  • A raised, itchy rash that follows only one colour. That can be a reaction to a pigment.

You are welcome to message us a photo any time, and we will tell you honestly whether what you are seeing is ordinary. But do not wait on a reply from us if you have a fever or spreading redness. An infection caught early is a small problem; left alone it is not.

Sun is the long game

Once the tattoo has healed, UV becomes the main thing that ages it. Sunlight breaks pigment particles into fragments small enough for your body to carry away, and that is what fading actually is.

SPF 30 or higher, every time the tattoo is in the sun, for as long as you have it. This matters most on fine lines and light grey work, where there is less pigment to lose in the first place. We go into it properly in Do fine line tattoos fade?

Fine line tattoos need a lighter hand

Thin lines sit in a narrow channel and carry less ink than a bold outline, so they are less forgiving while they heal. Over-moisturising, scratching and sunburn all do more visible damage to a fine line piece than to a heavy traditional one. Same routine, just less cream and more patience.

Healing while you are travelling

If you were tattooed in Prague and fly home a day or two later, nothing changes about the routine. Keep your soap and cream in your hand luggage, keep the area out of the sun, and avoid placements that sit under a seatbelt or a bag strap on the way home. Our guide for visitors covers flying, swimming and timing a tattoo around a trip.

Touch-ups

Sometimes a small patch lifts during peeling and heals lighter than the rest. That is common, it is not a mistake, and it is quick to correct.

Wait until the tattoo is fully healed, at least four to six weeks, then send us a photo. We will tell you whether it actually needs anything and arrange a time if it does. Judging it earlier, during the milky stage, usually leads people to ask for a touch-up they do not need.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a tattoo take to heal?

The surface closes in about two to four weeks. The deeper layers carry on settling for around three months, which is why a tattoo keeps looking better after the first month.

Can I shower with a new tattoo?

Yes. Short, lukewarm showers are fine from day one. No baths, no pools, nothing that soaks it until the skin has closed.

Why does my tattoo look faded after two weeks?

New skin has formed over the ink and it is not transparent yet. The colour comes back between weeks five and eight.

Is itching normal?

Yes, especially in week two. Tap the area or add a thin layer of cream instead of scratching. Scratching pulls ink out with the scab.

What cream should I use?

A thin, fragrance-free moisturiser, or the healing cream we recommend. Avoid thick petroleum ointments, which keep the scab wet.

When can I go swimming?

After three to four weeks, once it has stopped peeling. That includes pools, lakes, the sea and hot tubs.

When can I train again?

Light exercise after a few days. Keep sweat, friction and heavy stretching off the area for about a week.

How do I know if it is infected?

Spreading redness, pain or swelling increasing after day three, heat, thick yellow or green discharge, or fever. Any of those means see a doctor rather than wait.

Not sure what you are looking at?

Send us a photo. We would far rather answer a question about a tattoo that is healing perfectly well than hear about it three weeks later.
